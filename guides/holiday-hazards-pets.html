Holiday Pet Hazards: Thanksgiving to New Year

Rich food on low tables, cookie tins under the tree, new plants, guests with handbags full of pills, and a lot of distraction make the stretch from Thanksgiving to New Year a busy time for pet poisonings. Here is what to do if your dog or cat got into something, which holiday hazards are dangerous, and which calculator to use.

Call now if your pet got into something this holiday: ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435 Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661 Find a 24/7 emergency vet near me Both hotlines are open 24/7, including holidays. A consultation fee may apply. This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. Medical disclaimer.

What to do right now

  1. Move your pet away and pick up everything that is left, including wrappers, packaging, and plant pieces.
  2. Keep the label, box, recipe, or a photo of the plant. Check for chocolate type, xylitol (also listed as birch sugar, birch extract, or wood sugar), raisins, nuts, onion, garlic, and alcohol.
  3. Estimate how much is missing and note the time it happened and your pet's weight.
  4. Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control center, even if your pet seems normal. Do not make your pet vomit unless a professional tells you to.
  5. If you see string, ribbon, or tinsel in the mouth or under the tongue, do not pull it. Call your vet.

Symptoms to watch for

  • Vomiting, diarrhea, or a swollen, painful belly (rich food, yeast dough, plants)
  • Restlessness, racing heart, tremors, or seizures (chocolate, macadamia nuts, some plants)
  • Weakness, wobbliness, or collapse (xylitol, alcohol, macadamia nuts)
  • Not eating or lethargy (raisins, pancreatitis, a blockage)
  • Not pooping or straining to poop (tinsel, ribbon, bones, ornaments)
  • Drooling, pawing at the mouth, or trouble breathing (liquid potpourri, batteries, holly)
  • Vomiting, drooling, hiding, or not eating in a cat that may have touched a lily (do not wait for signs, call now)

Some problems show up hours or days later, so a pet that seems fine right after eating something can still need care.

Holiday dangers for cats

Cats are the most likely to play with and swallow tinsel, ribbon, and gift string, which can cause a life-threatening string-type blockage. Even a few licks of liquid potpourri or simmering scented oil can burn a cat's mouth. Lilies are common in holiday bouquets, and any contact with a true lily or daylily, including pollen and vase water, is an emergency for cats. Inspect bouquets that guests bring before they come inside.

Holiday safety checklist

  • Keep your pet's diet the same. Ask guests not to share plates, and put leftovers and carcasses in a closed trash can.
  • Do not leave rising dough, drinks, or dessert trays where pets can reach them.
  • Ask guests to keep medicine in a closed cabinet, not in a suitcase or handbag on the floor.
  • Do not put wrapped food or candy under the tree. Pets can smell it through the paper.
  • Secure the tree so it cannot tip, cover the tree water, and skip tinsel if you have a cat.
  • Keep wires, batteries, glass ornaments, and food-based ornaments out of reach.
  • Choose artificial versions of lilies, mistletoe, and holly, or keep real ones well out of reach.
  • Never leave lit candles or a menorah unattended, and keep simmer pots of potpourri away from cats.
  • Give your pet a quiet room during parties and New Year's Eve noise, and check ID tags and microchip details.
  • Save both poison control numbers in your phone, and find your nearest 24/7 emergency vet now, since many regular clinics close for the holidays.

Frequently asked questions

Are poinsettias poisonous to dogs and cats?

Poinsettias are only mildly toxic. Pet Poison Helpline says eating them usually causes drooling, lip licking, vomiting, or rarely diarrhea, and the sap can irritate skin and eyes. Signs usually settle on their own. Call your vet if they are severe or do not stop.

Is mistletoe or holly dangerous for pets?

Yes. A few leaves or berries usually cause stomach upset, but larger amounts of mistletoe can affect heart rate and blood pressure, and holly leaves can injure the mouth. Call your vet or a poison control line if your pet ate any.

My cat ate tinsel or ribbon. What should I do?

Call your vet now and do not pull on any string you can see in the mouth or under the tongue. Tinsel, ribbon, and yarn can cause a string-type blockage that can cut through the intestines and often needs surgery.

My dog ate raw bread or roll dough. Is that an emergency?

It can be. ASPCA Poison Control says raw yeast dough can expand in the stomach, cause painful bloating that may twist the stomach, and produce alcohol as it ferments. Call your vet or a poison control line right away.

Can I give my dog turkey or ham at the holidays?

ASPCA Poison Control recommends keeping your pet's diet the same. Fatty, sweet, and spicy foods and cooked bones can all be dangerous, and rich fatty food can trigger pancreatitis. A small piece of plain boneless turkey is the most they suggest.
