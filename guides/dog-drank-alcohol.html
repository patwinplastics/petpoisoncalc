My Dog or Cat Drank Alcohol: What to Do Now

A beer left on the coffee table, a spilled glass of wine, a lick of hand sanitizer or vanilla extract: pets get drunk much faster than people, and alcohol can drop their blood sugar and body temperature. Here is how much is too much, and what to do.

If your dog or cat drank beer, wine, liquor, or licked hand sanitizer, mouthwash, or an extract, call an animal poison control center or your vet now, even if your pet seems fine. Go straight to an emergency vet if your pet is wobbly, very sleepy, cold, hard to wake, or is a cat, a puppy, or a kitten. Use the alcohol calculator to estimate the dose.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Take away the drink or product and keep your pet warm and quiet.
  2. Check the label for the alcohol %, and estimate how much is missing. Note the time.
  3. Call ASPCA Animal Poison Control, Pet Poison Helpline, or your vet.
  4. Do not make your pet vomit unless a professional tells you to. Alcohol is absorbed within about an hour.
  5. Go to an emergency vet if your pet is wobbly, very sleepy, cold to the touch, or hard to wake.

How Much Alcohol Is Toxic?

There is no published safe amount. In a published case report, an 18 kg (40 lb) dog that drank about 2 oz of 40% rum, roughly 1 gram of pure alcohol per kg of body weight, was in a drunken stupor (Houston and Head, 1993). An ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center review lists a lethal oral dose of 5.5 to 7.9 g per kg in dogs.

The alcohol calculator uses those figures: under 1 g per kg is Urgent and 1 g per kg or more is an Emergency for dogs. There is no lower tier, because a dose that causes no signs has not been published. Cats have no established toxic dose, so any amount is an emergency.

Where Alcohol Hides

  • Drinks: beer, cider, hard seltzer, wine, liquor, cocktails, and punch. Creamy drinks like eggnog and cream liqueurs are extra tempting.
  • Hand sanitizer: many are 60 to 70% alcohol, so a few licks can be a large dose for a small pet. Some contain isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol instead, which is different. Call poison control.
  • Extracts and mouthwash: vanilla and other extracts and mouthwash contain alcohol too.
  • Desserts: rum-soaked cakes and boozy desserts. Sweet drinks and desserts may also contain xylitol or chocolate.
  • Raw yeast dough: makes alcohol in the stomach. See raw bread dough.

Dogs vs Cats: What Is Different

  • Dose data: the published figures are from dogs. For cats, treat any amount as an emergency.
  • Size: cats, puppies, kittens, and toy breeds reach a high dose with very little, especially with liquor, sanitizer, or extracts.
  • How they get it: dogs drink from glasses and cans left within reach. Cats more often lick spills or sanitizer from skin and fur.

Symptoms and Timeline

  • Within an hour: vomiting, drooling, wobbly walking, and restlessness that turns into sleepiness.
  • 1 to 6 hours: heavy sleepiness or collapse, low body temperature, low blood sugar, and slow breathing.
  • Severe cases: seizures, coma, and breathing failure.
  • Recovery: most pets with mild signs recover within 12 to 24 hours with care.

Go Straight to an Emergency Vet If

  • Your pet is wobbly, very sleepy, cold to the touch, or hard to wake
  • Your pet is a cat, a puppy, a kitten, or very small
  • The drink was liquor, hand sanitizer, or an extract
  • The calculator shows Emergency

What the Vet May Do

Alcohol is absorbed within about an hour, so making a pet vomit is rarely helpful once signs start. Treatment focuses on keeping blood sugar, body temperature, and breathing stable, with monitoring until your pet sobers up. That is why calling right away matters.

How to Prevent It

  • Keep drinks off low tables, and pick up glasses and cans right after a party.
  • Store hand sanitizer, mouthwash, and extracts in a closed cabinet.
  • Let sanitizer dry fully before you pet a cat or let a dog lick your hands.
  • Never give a pet alcohol as a joke. Their bodies cannot handle it.

Frequently Asked Questions

My dog drank some beer. What should I do?

Call your vet or a poison control line now, even if your dog seems fine. Keep your dog warm and quiet, and do not make your dog vomit unless you are told to. Go in right away if your dog is wobbly, very sleepy, or hard to wake.

How much alcohol is toxic to a dog?

There is no published safe amount. A medium-size dog was in a drunken stupor after about 1 gram of pure alcohol per kg, roughly two ounces of rum for an 18 kg (40 lb) dog. The lowest published lethal dose is 5.5 g per kg. Small dogs reach these doses with very little liquor.

My dog licked a little wine. Is that an emergency?

It depends on your dog's size and how much was swallowed, so enter both in the alcohol calculator. A lick is a small dose for a large dog, but more than a lick can matter for a small dog or a cat. Call poison control if you are unsure.

Is hand sanitizer dangerous for pets?

Many hand sanitizers are 60 to 70% alcohol, so a few licks can be a large dose for a small pet. Some contain isopropyl alcohol instead. Check the label and call poison control.

Is alcohol more dangerous for cats?

Cats are small, and there is no established toxic dose for them, so any amount a cat swallows is treated as an emergency.
