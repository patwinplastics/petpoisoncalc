My Dog Ate Halloween Candy: What to Do Now

Candy bowls by the door, trick-or-treat bags on the floor, and a busy night of visitors make Halloween one of the most common times for pets to get into something they should not. Here is what to do if your dog or cat raided the candy, which treats are dangerous, and how to keep Halloween night safe.

Call now if your pet got into Halloween candy: ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435 Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661 Both are open 24/7. A consultation fee may apply. This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. Medical disclaimer.

What to do right now

  1. Move your pet away from the candy and pick up everything that is left, including wrappers.
  2. Gather the bags and wrappers. Count what is left to estimate what is missing. Missing wrappers may mean they were swallowed too.
  3. Check labels for chocolate type, xylitol (also listed as birch sugar, birch extract, or wood sugar), raisins, nuts, and THC.
  4. Note your pet's weight and the time it happened.
  5. Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control center, even if your pet seems normal. Do not make your pet vomit unless a professional tells you to.

Symptoms to watch for after a candy raid

  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Restlessness, racing heart, tremors, or seizures (chocolate)
  • Weakness, wobbliness, or collapse (xylitol, THC, macadamia nuts)
  • Not eating, belly pain, or lethargy (raisins, pancreatitis)
  • Not pooping or straining to poop (swallowed wrappers or sticks)
  • Heavy drooling or foaming at the mouth (glow sticks)

Some problems show up hours or days later, so a pet that seems fine right after eating candy can still need care.

Halloween dangers for cats

Cats eat candy less often than dogs, but chocolate still affects them, and their small size means less is needed to cause concern. Cats are the most common glow stick chewers. Ribbon, string, and costume ties can cause a dangerous string-type blockage if swallowed. Lilies in autumn arrangements are an emergency for cats: call right away for any contact.

Halloween night safety checklist

  • Keep candy bowls and trick-or-treat bags in closed containers up high, not on coffee tables or the floor.
  • Check kids' candy hauls for chocolate, sugar-free candy, raisins, and anything that could be an edible, and ask kids not to stash candy in their rooms.
  • Put pets in a quiet room or crate during trick-or-treating so they cannot slip out the door.
  • Make sure ID tags and microchip details are current.
  • Keep candles, jack-o'-lanterns, glow sticks, and battery-powered decor out of reach.
  • Only use pet costumes that do not limit sight, movement, or breathing, with no small parts to chew off. Never leave a costumed pet alone.
  • Throw out carved pumpkins before they mold.
  • Save both poison control numbers in your phone tonight.

Frequently asked questions

What Halloween candy is toxic to dogs?

The biggest concerns are chocolate (especially dark and baking chocolate), sugar-free candy and gum that contain xylitol, raisins (including chocolate-covered raisins), and THC edibles that look like regular candy. Large amounts of any sugary or fatty candy can cause pancreatitis, and swallowed wrappers can cause a bowel blockage.

My dog ate a fun-size chocolate bar. What should I do?

Note your dog's weight, the type of chocolate, and how many pieces are missing, then use the chocolate calculator and call your vet or a poison control line. Risk depends on chocolate type, amount, and your dog's size, so there is no single number of fun-size bars that is okay.

My dog ate candy wrappers. Is that dangerous?

It can be. Foil, cellophane, and lollipop sticks can cause a bowel blockage that may need surgery. Count what is left to estimate how many wrappers are missing, and call your vet. Watch for vomiting, not eating, lethargy, or straining to poop.

Is candy corn bad for dogs?

Candy corn is not toxic, but it is almost pure sugar. A few pieces may upset the stomach, and a large amount can cause vomiting, diarrhea, or pancreatitis, which can show up 2 to 4 days later. Call your vet if your dog ate a lot.

My cat bit a glow stick. What should I do?

Glow stick liquid tastes very bitter and can cause heavy drooling, foaming, and mouth irritation. It is not usually life-threatening. Wipe the fur and mouth area and call your vet or a poison control line if signs do not settle or your cat swallowed pieces.
