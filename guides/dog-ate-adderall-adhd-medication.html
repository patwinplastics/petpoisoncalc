My Dog or Cat Ate Adderall or Other ADHD Medication: What to Do Now

A dropped pill, a chewed bottle, a backpack left on the floor: stimulant ADHD medicines act fast in pets, and even part of one pill can cause signs in a small dog. Here is what to know and what to do.

If your pet ate an ADHD pill or patch: ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435 Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661 Find a 24/7 emergency vet near me Both hotlines are open 24/7. A consultation fee may apply. This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. Medical disclaimer.

If your dog or cat swallowed or chewed an ADHD pill or patch, call an animal poison control center or your vet now. Signs can start within 15 to 30 minutes. Go straight to an emergency vet if your pet is pacing, agitated, panting hard, trembling, or very hot, or if your pet is a cat. Use the ADHD medication calculator to estimate the dose.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Take away any pills, the bottle, or the patch, and keep your pet in a quiet, dim, cool room.
  2. Check the label for the drug name, the mg per pill, and whether it is extended-release (XR, ER, CD, LA). Count what is missing.
  3. Call ASPCA Animal Poison Control, Pet Poison Helpline, or your vet.
  4. Do not make your pet vomit unless a professional tells you to, and do not give any sedative or calming medicine from home.
  5. Go to an emergency vet if your pet is pacing, agitated, trembling, very hot, or has a seizure.

How Much Is Toxic?

Very little. The Merck Veterinary Manual chapter on amphetamines and ADHD drugs, written with Pet Poison Helpline, reports restlessness and agitation in dogs at about 0.09 mg of amphetamine per kg, overheating and a fast heart rate at 0.2 mg per kg, tremors at 0.7 mg per kg, and seizures at 1.3 mg per kg. For methylphenidate, overheating and a fast heart rate have been reported at 0.3 mg per kg.

The ADHD medication calculator treats amphetamine at 0.09 mg per kg or more, or methylphenidate at 0.3 mg per kg or more, as an Emergency, and lower doses as Urgent. For most dogs, that means nearly any whole amphetamine pill is an Emergency. There is no lower tier.

Non-stimulant ADHD medicines work differently and are not covered by this dose math. Treat any amount as an emergency and call poison control.

Dogs vs Cats: What Is Different

  • How they get it: dogs chew through pill bottles and eat pills dropped on the floor.
  • Dose data: the published doses are from dogs. Cats have no established cutoff, so any amount is an emergency.
  • Signs in cats: cats may sit very still and stare instead of acting hyper, but their heart rate and temperature can still rise.

Symptoms and Timeline

  • 15 minutes to a few hours: restlessness, pacing, circling, dilated pupils, panting, vocalizing, and agitation.
  • Next hours: a fast heart rate, high blood pressure, overheating, and tremors.
  • Severe cases: seizures and collapse.
  • How long: immediate-release pills act within about 30 minutes. Extended-release pills and patches can start later, and signs can last 24 to 72 hours.

Go Straight to an Emergency Vet If

  • Your pet is pacing, agitated, trembling, or very hot
  • Your pet has a seizure or collapses
  • Your pet is a cat, or the calculator shows Emergency
  • The pill was extended-release or a patch, or you do not know what it was

What the Vet May Do

Because signs can start within minutes, vets often treat the signs directly: medicines to calm agitation and slow a racing heart, cooling for a high temperature, IV fluids, and monitoring until the drug wears off. If the pill was swallowed very recently and your pet has no signs, the vet may make your pet vomit.

How to Prevent It

  • Keep ADHD medicine in its bottle, in a closed cabinet or drawer, not on a nightstand or desk.
  • Ask family members and guests to keep backpacks, purses, and pill organizers up high or in a closed room.
  • Take pills over a counter, and pick up any dropped pill right away.
  • Dispose of used patches folded in half in a lidded bin.

Frequently Asked Questions

My dog ate an Adderall. What should I do?

Call poison control or your vet now, even if your dog seems fine. Signs can start within 15 to 30 minutes. Keep your dog in a quiet, cool room, do not give any calming medicine from home, and go in right away if your dog is pacing, panting hard, or trembling.

How much Adderall is toxic to a dog?

Restlessness has been reported at about 0.09 mg of amphetamine per kg. For a 40 lb (18 kg) dog that is under 2 mg, so a single 5 mg tablet is already above it. Seizures have been reported at 1.3 mg per kg.

My dog ate a Vyvanse capsule. Is it different?

Vyvanse is a form of amphetamine the body converts after swallowing. The calculator counts the full mg on the label, which may overestimate a little but keeps the result cautious. Call poison control either way.

Is Ritalin or Concerta less dangerous?

Methylphenidate products have their own published dose, with overheating and a fast heart rate reported in dogs at about 0.3 mg per kg. A single 10 mg tablet reaches that dose in a dog up to about 73 lb (33 kg). Call poison control either way.

Is ADHD medication more dangerous for cats?

Cats have no established cutoff, so any amount is treated as an emergency. Cats may sit still and stare instead of acting hyper, so do not wait for obvious signs.
