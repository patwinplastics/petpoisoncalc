Pet Poison Control: Phone Numbers, Cost, and When to Call

There are two 24/7 animal poison control lines in the US. Both charge a fee, both include call backs on the same case, and both are worth the call. Here are the numbers, what each one costs, and what to have ready.

Pet ate something toxic? Call now: ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435 Pet Poison Helpline (855) 764-7661 Find a 24/7 emergency vet near me Both hotlines are open 24/7. A consultation fee may apply. This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. Medical disclaimer.

If your pet is having trouble breathing, having a seizure, collapsed, or will not wake up, go to the nearest emergency vet now and call on the way. Otherwise, call one of the lines above or your own vet. Do not give home remedies, and do not make your pet vomit unless a vet or the hotline tells you to (Pet Poison Helpline).

Is the fee worth it?

Often, yes. The ASPCA says a one-time consultation fee can help you avoid thousands in emergency vet costs, and Pet Poison Helpline says the sooner an exposure is handled, the easier, cheaper, and safer it is to treat. Many calls end with "watch at home," and when your pet does need care, the hotline can give your vet a treatment plan. AAHA notes much of the fee can be covered if your pet has pet health insurance, so check your policy.

What to have ready when you call

  • Your pet's species, weight, age, and any health problems or medicines
  • What your pet got into, with the package, label, or bottle in hand (or a photo)
  • How much is missing, at most, and about what time it happened
  • Any signs so far, such as vomiting, drooling, shaking, or acting wobbly
  • Your vet's or the nearest emergency clinic's phone number
  • A card for the fee

Calling back about the same case

Planning for the vet bill that may follow? See pet insurance, emergency vet costs, and vet telehealth.

Write down the case number they give you. If signs change, you have a question later, or you end up at a clinic, call the same number and give the case number. The ASPCA says there is no charge for follow-up calls on an established case, though a new exposure may be a new case. Pet Poison Helpline includes all follow-up consultations in its fee. An emergency vet may also ask you to call so its team can talk with the toxicology staff (ASPCA).

If you live in Georgia

AAHA reported that since July 1, 2025, a Georgia law (Senate Bill 105) has kept both hotlines from giving risk assessments or treatment advice directly to Georgia pet owners. They can still help through your veterinarian, so call your vet or an emergency clinic first.

Frequently asked questions

Is pet poison control free?

No. Pet Poison Helpline charges a $99 fee per incident, and ASPCA Animal Poison Control says a consultation fee may apply. Do not let the fee stop the call: both lines say early advice can avoid bigger vet bills.

How much does ASPCA Animal Poison Control cost?

The ASPCA's own page says only that a consultation fee may apply and does not list an amount. AAHA reported a $95 fee in 2025. Ask about the fee at the start of the call.

How much does Pet Poison Helpline cost?

Pet Poison Helpline's website lists a $99 per incident fee, and all follow-up consultations on that case are included.

Do I pay again if I call poison control back?

Usually not for the same case. The ASPCA says there is no charge for follow-up calls on an established case, though charges may apply for new cases. Pet Poison Helpline includes all follow-up consultations in its per-incident fee. Keep your case number.

What is the ASPCA poison control call back number?

Call the same number, (888) 426-4435, and give the case number from your first call. Your vet can also call with that case number to talk with the toxicology team.

Should I call poison control or go to the emergency vet?

If your pet is having trouble breathing, having a seizure, collapsed, or will not wake up, go to the nearest emergency vet now and call on the way. If your pet seems normal, call poison control or your vet first; they will tell you whether your pet needs to be seen.
