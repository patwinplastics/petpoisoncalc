Did Your Dog or Cat Drink Alcohol? Check the Risk.

Estimate how much pure alcohol your pet swallowed for its size, and how urgently to call a vet.

Last reviewed November 27, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Pets get drunk much faster than people, and alcohol can drop their blood sugar and body temperature.

Beer, wine, liquor, hand sanitizer, mouthwash, and vanilla extract all contain alcohol. Dogs have become stuporous at about 1 g of alcohol per kg, and there is no established cutoff for cats. This tool never calls an amount safe. Contact a veterinarian or animal poison control after any amount.

Emergency Vet or Poison Control: Which Do I Call?

Alcohol is absorbed within about an hour, so vomiting is rarely helpful once signs start. Treatment focuses on blood sugar, body temperature, and breathing.

Go Straight to an Emergency Vet If…

  • Your pet is wobbly, very sleepy, cold to the touch, or hard to wake
  • Your pet is a cat, a puppy, a kitten, or very small
  • The drink was liquor, hand sanitizer, or an extract
  • Poison control or your vet has told you to come in

Call Poison Control First If…

  • Your pet just drank a small amount and seems normal
  • You are unsure how much was swallowed
  • You want to know whether to watch at home or go in
  • You want a case number to hand to your vet
Do not try to make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to.

How Alcohol Poisoning Develops

Signs usually start within an hour. Most pets with mild signs recover within 12 to 24 hours with care.

0 to 1 hour

Early signs

  • Vomiting, drooling
  • Wobbly walking
  • Restlessness, then sleepiness
1 to 6 hours

Getting worse

  • Heavy sleepiness, collapse
  • Low body temperature
  • Low blood sugar, slow breathing
Severe cases

Life-threatening

  • Seizures or coma
  • Breathing failure
Drinks with milk or cream, like eggnog or cream liqueurs, are extra tempting to pets. Sweet drinks may also contain xylitol or chocolate.

Published Data Behind These Estimates

Published dose data comes from dogs. These figures are from the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center, a published case report, and a veterinary review.

Alcohol dose (dogs) Reported effect
about 1 g/kg Case report: an 18 kg dog that drank 2 oz of 40% rum (about 1 g/kg) was in a drunken stupor
5.5 to 7.9 g/kg Published oral lethal dose of pure alcohol in dogs (ASPCA APCC review)
Cats No established toxic dose

How this calculator sorts results: grams of pure alcohol = mL × alcohol % × 0.789. Dogs under 1 g/kg = Urgent; 1 g/kg and above = Emergency. Any amount for a cat, or any pet showing signs, = Emergency. There is no lower tier because a dose that causes no signs has not been published.

Common Questions

How much alcohol is toxic to a dog?

There is no published safe amount. A medium-size dog was in a drunken stupor after about 1 gram of pure alcohol per kg, roughly two ounces of rum for an 18 kg (40 lb) dog. The lowest published lethal dose is 5.5 g/kg. Small dogs reach these doses with very little liquor.

My dog drank some beer. What should I do?

Call your vet or a poison control line now, even if your dog seems fine. Keep your dog warm and quiet, and do not make your dog vomit unless you are told to. Go in right away if your dog is wobbly, very sleepy, or hard to wake.

Is hand sanitizer dangerous for pets?

Many hand sanitizers are 60 to 70% alcohol, so a few licks can be a large dose for a small pet. Some contain isopropyl alcohol instead. Check the label and call poison control.

Is alcohol more dangerous for cats?

Cats are small, and there is no established toxic dose for them, so this page treats any amount a cat swallows as an emergency.
