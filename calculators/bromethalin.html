Did your dog or cat eat bromethalin rat poison? Estimate the dose.

Bromethalin is a common nerve-acting rat and mouse poison, usually sold as 0.01% bait blocks, pellets, or bait stations. Enter your pet's weight, the concentration on the label, and how much bait is missing to estimate the dose compared with published veterinary toxic doses.

Last reviewed October 29, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Every bromethalin ingestion is an emergency. There is no antidote.

Bromethalin causes swelling of the brain, has a narrow margin of safety, and is not treated with vitamin K1. Pet Poison Helpline says prompt treatment is often needed in all species, and cats are more sensitive than dogs. Signs can be delayed for days, so do not wait for them. This tool estimates the dose for your phone call; it cannot rule out risk.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Because there is no antidote, getting the bait out of the stomach early is the main treatment. Merck Veterinary Manual recommends inducing vomiting if the ingestion was within 4 hours and the pet has no signs, followed by multiple doses of activated charcoal. Decontamination should be done by a vet, who also watches sodium levels while charcoal is given.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your pet ate any bromethalin bait, especially in the last 4 hours
  • Your pet is wobbly, weak in the back legs, trembling, overly excited, depressed, or having a seizure
  • Your pet is a cat, a puppy or kitten, or very small
  • You cannot tell how much bait is missing

Call poison control first if…

  • You are not sure the poison is bromethalin and need help reading the label
  • You found the bait days ago and your pet seems normal
  • You want a case number and a treatment plan to hand to your vet
Do not make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to, and never if your pet is wobbly, trembling, or very sleepy. Bring the package or a photo of the label. If another pet may also have eaten bait, have it checked too.

How bromethalin poisoning develops

Bromethalin is absorbed quickly and converted in the body into a compound that disrupts energy production in brain and nerve cells. Fluid builds up in the brain and spinal cord, which causes the signs below. The timing and type of signs depend on the dose.

4 to 36 hours

Convulsant syndrome (larger doses in dogs)

  • Hyperexcitability, muscle tremors
  • Seizures, high body temperature
  • Can be fatal
1 to 5 days

Paralytic syndrome (smaller doses, and cats)

  • Wobbly walk, back leg weakness
  • Depression, odd behavior
  • Paralysis
Up to 7 days or longer

Delayed and lasting effects

  • Signs can first appear days after eating the bait
  • Recovery from nerve damage can take weeks
  • Some damage may be permanent
Timing from Merck Veterinary Manual. Cats typically develop the paralytic form regardless of the dose. Pet Poison Helpline notes the effects of bromethalin can be serious and long-lasting.

Published data behind these estimates

These figures are from Merck Veterinary Manual and ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center publications. LD50 means the dose that killed half the animals in a study. Severe poisoning has been reported in pets at doses below the LD50, so there is no safe amount.

Dose or fact Documented effect
Dogs: about 0.24 mg/kg Toxic dose, taken as one tenth of the lowest reported LD50 (Merck)
Dogs: 2.38 to 5.6 mg/kg Reported LD50 range (Merck)
Dogs: about 3.54 mg/kg Average LD50; doses at or above this may cause seizures within 4 to 36 hours (Merck)
Cats: about 0.04 mg/kg Toxic dose, one tenth of the lowest reported LD50 (Merck)
Cats: 0.4 to 0.71 mg/kg Reported LD50 range (Merck). Cats are the most sensitive pets.

Common questions

Is there an antidote for bromethalin?

No. Unlike anticoagulant rat poisons, bromethalin is not treated with vitamin K1 and has no antidote. Treatment is early decontamination (vomiting and activated charcoal) and supportive care to reduce brain swelling, which is why the first few hours matter so much.

How much bromethalin is toxic to a dog?

Merck Veterinary Manual says toxic doses are widely accepted at one tenth of the lowest reported LD50. For dogs that is about 0.24 mg of bromethalin per kg. With a 0.01% bait (0.1 mg per gram), that is about 2.4 g of bait per kg, or roughly 11 g for a 10 lb dog.

How much bromethalin is toxic to a cat?

Cats are much more sensitive. Their LD50 is 0.4 to 0.71 mg per kg, so the toxic dose is about 0.04 mg per kg. For a 10 lb cat that is under 2 g of a 0.01% bait, a small fraction of one block.

When do symptoms of bromethalin poisoning start?

Signs can start within hours or be delayed up to 7 days. Large doses in dogs can cause tremors and seizures within 4 to 36 hours; smaller doses can cause weakness and paralysis 1 to 5 days later. Pets can look normal at first.

My pet ate rat poison but I do not know which kind. What now?

Call poison control and bring the package. Bromethalin, anticoagulants, cholecalciferol, and zinc phosphide are treated in completely different ways, and the active ingredient is printed on the label. The rat and mouse poison calculator walks through each type.
