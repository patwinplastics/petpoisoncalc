Did your dog or cat eat rock salt, ice melt, or salt dough? Check how much is toxic.

Estimate how urgently your dog or cat needs veterinary attention after eating rock salt or sidewalk ice melt, table salt, homemade play dough, or a salt dough ornament, based on species, body weight, the product, and how much was eaten.

Last reviewed November 11, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Too much salt can cause seizures, and salt dough is far saltier than it looks.

In dogs, signs of salt poisoning can appear after about 2 to 3 g of salt per kg of body weight, and about 4 g per kg can be lethal (Merck Veterinary Manual). There is no published toxic dose for cats. Ice melts made with calcium, potassium, or magnesium salts or urea have no published toxic dose either and can burn the mouth, stomach, and paws. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control. Never use salt or salt water to make a pet vomit.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Vomiting usually starts within a few hours of eating too much salt. Vets may make a dog vomit if it is safe and the dog has no signs yet, check blood sodium, and give IV fluids that lower sodium slowly over 2 to 3 days, because lowering it too fast can cause brain swelling (Merck Veterinary Manual). Ice melts that are not sodium chloride are treated for irritation: stomach protectants, fluids, and checks on potassium, magnesium, or calcium.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your pet is vomiting repeatedly, very thirsty, wobbly, shaking, confused, weak, or having a seizure
  • Your cat ate any salt, salt dough, or ice melt
  • Your pet ate a salt dough ornament or a chunk of play dough
  • Your pet has blood in vomit or stool, or mouth sores, after eating ice melt
  • The estimated dose is in the Emergency range

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog licked a treated sidewalk or its paws and seems normal
  • You are not sure what kind of ice melt it was or how much is missing
  • Your pet has heart or kidney disease, which raises the risk from salt
  • You want a case number and treatment plan to hand to your vet
Do not make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to, and never use salt or salt water to do it. Pet Poison Helpline warns that salt used to cause vomiting can itself cause salt poisoning. Offer fresh water in small amounts, and do not force your pet to drink.

How salt poisoning develops

Too much sodium in the blood pulls water out of brain cells. The faster sodium rises, the worse the signs (ASPCA Toxicology Brief). Ice melt can also irritate or burn the mouth, stomach, skin, and paw pads.

Within about 3 hours

Early signs

  • Vomiting, often the first sign
  • Heavy thirst and peeing a lot
  • Diarrhea, drooling, not eating
Next few hours

Nervous system

  • Lethargy, wobbliness, disorientation
  • Muscle tremors and twitching
  • Fast heart rate and fast breathing
Large doses

Severe salt poisoning

  • Seizures
  • Collapse or coma
  • Death is possible without treatment
Timing is from the Merck Veterinary Manual and Today's Veterinary Nurse. Pets with heart or kidney disease, and pets without access to fresh water, are at higher risk (ASPCA Toxicology Brief, 2002).

Published data behind these estimates

These figures come from veterinary toxicology references and ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center publications. Almost all salt dose data is from dogs. They describe documented effects, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous."

Dose or product Documented effect or content
2 to 3 g/kg (dogs) Clinical signs of salt toxicosis can appear (Merck Veterinary Manual). Today's Veterinary Nurse gives 2 g/kg.
About 4 g/kg (dogs) Acute oral lethal dose of salt in dogs (Merck Veterinary Manual; Pet Poison Helpline; ASPCA Toxicology Brief)
Cats No cat-specific toxic dose published. Dogs and cats that eat a lot of sodium chloride ice melt usually vomit, and high blood sodium is possible (ASPCA Toxicology Brief, 2002).
Table or rock salt About 6 g per teaspoon, 18 g per tablespoon, 292 g per cup (USDA FoodData Central)
Homemade play dough Some recipes hold about 8 g of salt per tablespoon of dough (Today's Veterinary Nurse)
Calcium chloride, CMA, calcium carbonate Most severe irritants in ice melts; stomach upset and skin and paw irritation. No published toxic dose (Pet Poison Helpline).
Potassium chloride Severe irritant, can cause bloody vomiting or diarrhea. No published toxic dose (Pet Poison Helpline).
Magnesium chloride, urea Usually drooling and stomach upset; large amounts can cause high magnesium, weakness, tremors, or methemoglobinemia (Pet Poison Helpline; ASPCA Toxicology Brief)

This calculator uses, for dogs: 2 g of salt per kg and above = Emergency, with a lethal-range warning at 4 g/kg and above. Below 2 g/kg = Urgent. Any ice melt that is not sodium chloride, any ice melt of unknown type, and any unknown amount is shown as Emergency, because no toxic dose has been published for those. For cats, any amount is shown as Emergency, because no toxic dose has been published for cats. There is no "safe" result, and even the lowest result says to call now.

Common questions

How much salt is toxic to a dog?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says signs of salt poisoning can appear in dogs after about 2 to 3 g of salt per kg of body weight, and about 4 g per kg can be lethal. A teaspoon of table salt weighs about 6 g, so a 10 lb dog reaches 2 g per kg with about a teaspoon and a half. Call poison control for any large or unknown amount.

My dog ate ice melt. What should I do?

Check the bag for the ingredients, move your dog away from it, wipe the mouth and paws with a damp cloth, and offer fresh water. Then call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661. Do not make your dog vomit unless a professional tells you to, and never use salt water to cause vomiting.

Is pet-safe ice melt safe if my pet eats it?

No ice melt is completely safe to eat. Pet Poison Helpline notes that urea-based products are the ones usually labeled pet safe and mostly cause drooling and stomach upset, but large amounts can cause weakness, tremors, and blood changes. Calcium chloride is one of the harshest irritants. Call poison control if your pet eats any.

Are salt dough ornaments dangerous for dogs?

Yes. Salt dough and homemade play dough are mostly flour, water, and salt, and some recipes hold about 8 g of salt per tablespoon. ASPCA warns that even a small amount of salt dough could be dangerous, even from old ornaments. Call your vet or poison control right away.

How much salt is toxic to a cat?

There is no published toxic dose of salt for cats, so this calculator treats any salt, salt dough, or ice melt a cat swallows as an emergency. Cats also groom ice melt off their paws and fur, so wipe their paws after any time outdoors or in a garage.
