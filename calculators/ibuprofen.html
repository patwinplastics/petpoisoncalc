Did your dog eat ibuprofen (Advil)? Check the risk.

Estimate how urgently your dog needs veterinary attention after eating ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), based on body weight, tablet strength, and how many tablets were eaten.

Last reviewed October 15, 2026 by the PetPoisonCalc editorial team against the sources listed below.

Ibuprofen has a narrow margin of safety in dogs.

Even a single regular-strength tablet can be a significant dose for a small dog, and stomach ulcers have been reported in dogs given low doses over time. Older dogs and dogs with kidney disease can develop kidney injury at lower doses than listed here. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary toxicology data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control. This calculator covers ibuprofen only: naproxen (Aleve) and other NSAIDs have different, often much lower, toxic doses, so call poison control right away for those.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Treatment works best when started early, often before any signs appear. Vets commonly induce vomiting, give activated charcoal and stomach protectants, and start IV fluids to protect the kidneys at higher doses.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your dog is vomiting (especially blood), has black or tarry stool, is weak, wobbly, or having a seizure
  • The estimated dose is in the Emergency range
  • Your dog is older or has known kidney, liver, or stomach problems
  • A poison control consultation or your vet has told you to bring the dog in

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate the pills and seems normal
  • You are unsure of the tablet strength or how many were eaten
  • The product was a combination medicine (cold and flu, sleep aid) with other ingredients
  • You want a case number and treatment plan to hand to your vet
Do not try to make your dog vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to, and never give your dog any other pain medicine.

How ibuprofen toxicosis develops

Ibuprofen blocks the prostaglandins that protect the stomach lining and keep blood flowing to the kidneys. Higher doses add kidney and then nervous system effects.

Within hours – 24 hours

Stomach and gut signs

  • Vomiting, sometimes with blood
  • Diarrhea or black, tarry stool
  • Abdominal pain, loss of appetite
1 – 5 days

Kidney injury (higher doses)

  • Increased or decreased thirst and urination
  • Lethargy, poor appetite
  • Kidney values on bloodwork may rise before signs appear
Within hours (very high doses)

Nervous system signs

  • Wobbliness, depression
  • Seizures or coma
  • Shock
Stomach ulcers can develop over several days. A dog that seems fine the first day can still develop problems, so follow-up bloodwork is often recommended.

Published case data behind these estimates

These figures come from the Merck Veterinary Manual and ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center toxicology briefs. They describe documented clinical thresholds for a single acute ingestion, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous."

Single ibuprofen dose Documented clinical effect
≥ 25 mg/kg Vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, GI ulceration reported (ASPCA APCC)
≥ 175 mg/kg Increased risk of acute kidney injury, plus GI bleeding
> 400 mg/kg Seizures, wobbliness, coma, in addition to kidney and GI signs
> 600 mg/kg Potentially lethal

This calculator uses: under 25 mg/kg = contact a professional now; 25 to under 175 mg/kg = Urgent; 175 mg/kg and above = Emergency. Older dogs and dogs with kidney disease can be affected at lower doses.
