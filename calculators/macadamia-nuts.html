Did your dog eat macadamia nuts? Check the risk.

Estimate how urgently your dog needs veterinary attention after eating macadamia nuts, based on body weight and how many nuts were eaten.

Last reviewed October 15, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Macadamia nuts can make dogs weak and wobbly, and the products they come in are often more dangerous.

The toxic substance in macadamia nuts is unknown, and the lowest dose linked to signs varies between reports. Most dogs recover within 24–48 hours, but chocolate-covered nuts and baked goods can add chocolate or xylitol poisoning, and the high fat content can trigger pancreatitis. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary data. It cannot rule out risk; contact a veterinarian or animal poison control after any ingestion.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

A vet may recommend inducing vomiting if the nuts were eaten recently. Most dogs with signs recover with supportive care.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your dog cannot stand or walk normally, is trembling, or feels very hot
  • The nuts were chocolate-covered or in a product with xylitol
  • Your dog is very young, old, or has other health problems
  • Poison control or your vet has told you to come in

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate the nuts and seems normal
  • You are unsure how many were eaten
  • You want to know whether vomiting should be induced
  • You want a case number to hand to your vet
Do not try to make your dog vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to.

How macadamia nut toxicosis develops

Signs usually start within 12 hours and resolve within 24–48 hours. Hind-leg weakness is the classic sign.

1 – 12 hours

Onset

  • Weakness, especially in the back legs
  • Vomiting
  • Wobbliness, tremors
  • Fever (overheating)
12 – 24 hours

Peak

  • Difficulty standing
  • Depression, lethargy
24 – 48 hours

Recovery

  • Most dogs return to normal without lasting effects
  • Watch for vomiting and belly pain (possible pancreatitis) over several days
If the nuts were chocolate-covered, use the chocolate calculator too. Chocolate poisoning follows its own timeline and can be more serious.

Published case data behind these estimates

Published dose data is limited and varies between sources. These figures come from the Merck Veterinary Manual, an ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center review, and a peer-reviewed review article.

Macadamia dose Reported effect
0.7 g/kg Lowest dose associated with clinical signs in one report
2.2–2.4 g/kg Lowest doses with signs in ASPCA case data / Merck
> 1–2 g/kg Merck: inducing vomiting recommended if eaten recently and dog is normal
20 g/kg Experimental dose causing marked weakness and inability to stand within 12 hours

This calculator uses conservative tiers because the literature does not support precise cutoffs: under 0.7 g/kg = contact a professional now; 0.7 to under 20 g/kg = Urgent; 20 g/kg and above = Emergency. Chocolate-covered nuts and baked goods are always at least Urgent; anything with xylitol is Emergency.

Common questions

How serious are macadamia nuts for dogs?

Dogs that eat macadamia nuts can develop weakness, often in the back legs, plus vomiting, tremors, and a high temperature. Many recover with care, but published dose data is limited, so this page uses cautious thresholds and cannot call a small amount safe.

What if the nuts were covered in chocolate?

Count both problems. Chocolate adds theobromine risk on its own timeline, and baked goods can hide other ingredients. Use the chocolate calculator as well, and tell your vet about every ingredient you know about.

Should I make my dog vomit after macadamia nuts?

A veterinarian may recommend vomiting if the nuts were eaten recently and your dog is still acting normally. Do not start that at home unless they tell you to. Call first.
