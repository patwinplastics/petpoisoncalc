Did your dog eat a THC edible? Check the risk.

Estimate how urgently your dog needs veterinary attention after eating marijuana or a THC edible, based on body weight and the amount of THC on the label.

Last reviewed October 15, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

There is no "safe" amount of THC for a dog, and edibles often hide other toxins.

Dogs are far more sensitive to THC than people. Product labels are often inaccurate, and many edibles also contain chocolate, xylitol, or high-fat butter, which can be more dangerous than the THC itself. Published dose data for dogs is limited, so this tool uses conservative tiers. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control. Vets need an honest history to treat your dog, and they are there to help, not to judge.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Most dogs recover with supportive care, but signs can be severe with concentrates and large edibles, and other ingredients can change the picture entirely.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your dog is very sleepy, hard to wake, wobbly, trembling, dribbling urine, or having a seizure
  • The product was a concentrate (oil, wax, vape cartridge) or contained chocolate or xylitol
  • The estimated dose is in the Emergency range
  • Your dog has vomited and is very sedated (risk of inhaling vomit)

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate it and seems normal
  • You do not know how much THC was in the product
  • You want help deciding how urgently to act for your dog's weight
  • You want a case number to hand to your vet
Do not try to make your dog vomit at home. THC can reduce the ability to swallow and protect the airway, so vomiting can be dangerous without a vet.

How THC toxicosis develops

THC acts on cannabinoid receptors in the brain, and dogs have more of these receptors than people. Edibles are absorbed slowly, so signs can start late and last a long time.

30 minutes – 4 hours

Onset

  • Wobbliness, stumbling
  • Lethargy or unusual sleepiness, or sometimes agitation
  • Sensitivity to touch and sound, dilated pupils
  • Dribbling urine
Hours – 1 day

Peak effects

  • Vomiting
  • Slow heart rate, low body temperature
  • Tremors; with concentrates, possible seizures or coma
1 – 3 days

Recovery

  • Most dogs recover with supportive care
  • Edibles and concentrates can cause longer-lasting signs
If the edible contained chocolate or xylitol, those can cause separate, serious problems on their own timeline. Use the chocolate and xylitol calculators as well, and tell your vet.

Published case data behind these estimates

Published THC dose data for dogs is limited, and product labels are often inaccurate (one study found THC content correctly labeled in only 17% of products). These figures come from the Merck Veterinary Manual.

THC dose Documented clinical effect
0.3–0.5 mg/kg Mild clinical signs reported
> 2–3 mg/kg Moderate clinical signs reported
Concentrates, edibles with chocolate or xylitol Higher risk of severe signs; rare deaths reported after edibles

Because the literature does not support precise cutoffs, this calculator uses conservative tiers: under 0.3 mg/kg = contact a professional now; 0.3 to under 2 mg/kg = Urgent; 2 mg/kg and above = Emergency. Unknown THC amounts, concentrates, baked edibles, and edibles with chocolate are always at least Urgent. Edibles with xylitol or "sugar-free" products are always Emergency.

Common questions

What if I do not know how much THC was in the edible?

Use the best estimate you can, and say clearly that it is an estimate. Bring the package. Edibles vary widely, and some also contain chocolate or xylitol, which are separate emergencies. If the label is gone, tell the vet what the product looked like and how much is missing.

Why should I not make my dog vomit?

THC can make a dog sedated and less able to swallow safely. Vomiting in that state can lead to inhaling stomach contents. Do not induce vomiting at home. Call a veterinarian or poison control, and keep your dog from falling or wandering.

Is wobbliness a mild sign I can watch at home?

Wobbliness, urine leaking, and a low heart rate are common, and many dogs recover with supportive care, but some become severely sedated. This page cannot tell you that a given dog will be fine. Call now, especially if your dog is very hard to wake, is vomiting while sedated, or also ate chocolate or xylitol.
