Did your dog or cat eat coffee grounds, caffeine pills, or an energy drink? Check how much is toxic.

Estimate how urgently your dog or cat needs veterinary attention after eating coffee grounds or beans, swallowing a caffeine pill, or drinking coffee, tea, soda, or an energy drink, based on species, body weight, the product, and the amount.

Last reviewed November 25, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Caffeine can cause a racing heart, tremors, and seizures, and pills and beans are far stronger than coffee.

In dogs, signs of methylxanthine poisoning start at about 20 mg per kg of body weight, heart rhythm problems at 40 to 50 mg per kg, and seizures at 60 mg per kg or more (Merck Veterinary Manual). There is no established cutoff for cats, so any amount is treated as an emergency. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Caffeine is absorbed fast, and signs usually start within 1 to 2 hours. A vet may make a dog vomit if it is safe and the pet has no signs yet, give activated charcoal, and use IV fluids and heart monitoring. Treatment for signs can include medicine for tremors and a fast heart rate.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your pet is restless, panting hard, trembling, vomiting, or has a racing or uneven heartbeat
  • Your pet had a seizure or collapsed
  • Your pet swallowed a caffeine pill, pre-workout powder, or a mouthful of dry grounds or beans
  • Your pet is a cat
  • The estimated dose is in the Emergency range

Call poison control first if…

  • Your pet licked or drank a small amount of a drink and seems normal
  • You are not sure how much caffeine is in the product
  • Your pet has heart disease, seizures, or takes medicine
  • You want a case number and treatment plan to hand to your vet
Do not make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to. Keep your pet calm and quiet, and take the package, cup, or label with you.

How caffeine poisoning develops

Caffeine is a stimulant (a methylxanthine). It speeds up the heart and the nervous system, and a dog's body clears it slowly, with a half-life of about 4.5 hours (DeClementi, 2011). Signs can last 12 to 36 hours (Pet Poison Helpline).

Within 1 to 2 hours

Early signs

  • Restlessness, hyperactivity, panting
  • Vomiting and diarrhea
  • Drinking and peeing a lot
Next few hours

Heart and muscles

  • Fast or uneven heartbeat
  • Tremors, stiffness, wobbliness
  • High body temperature
Large doses

Severe caffeine poisoning

  • Seizures
  • Collapse or coma
  • Death is possible without treatment

Published data behind these estimates

These figures come from veterinary toxicology references and U.S. government food data. They describe documented effects, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous." Caffeine content varies a lot between products, so the calculator uses the high end of each published range.

Dose or product Documented effect or content
About 20 mg/kg (dogs) Mild signs: vomiting, diarrhea, extra thirst. This is the figure for methylxanthines in general, which includes caffeine (Merck Veterinary Manual).
40 to 50 mg/kg (dogs) Effects on the heart, including a fast or abnormal rhythm (Merck Veterinary Manual)
60 mg/kg and up (dogs) Seizures (Merck Veterinary Manual)
About 140 mg/kg (dogs) Reported lethal dose of caffeine in dogs (Gugler and colleagues, 2013; American College of Veterinary Pharmacists)
Cats Toxic doses of about 80 to 150 mg/kg are listed, and cats are slightly more sensitive than dogs (American College of Veterinary Pharmacists; Gugler and colleagues, 2013). No firm cutoff is set for signs.
Coffee beans and grounds About 280 to 570 mg of caffeine per ounce of dry beans (Gugler and colleagues, 2013)
Drinks, per 12 fl oz Regular brewed coffee 113 to 247 mg, energy drinks 41 to 246 mg, caffeinated soft drinks 23 to 83 mg, black tea 71 mg, green tea 37 mg (U.S. Food and Drug Administration)
Caffeine pills The mg per pill is on the label. Pet Poison Helpline notes that fatality is common when dogs eat caffeine pills.

This calculator uses, for dogs: 40 mg of caffeine per kg and above = Emergency, with a seizure-range warning at 60 mg/kg and above. Below 40 mg/kg = Urgent. Any unknown amount is shown as Emergency. For cats, any amount is shown as Emergency, because no cutoff has been established. The 20, 40, and 60 mg/kg figures are published for methylxanthines as a group and are applied to caffeine here. There is no "safe" result, and even the lowest result says to call now.

Common questions

How much caffeine is toxic to a dog?

Veterinary references report mild signs such as vomiting and restlessness at about 20 mg of methylxanthine per kg of body weight, heart rhythm problems at 40 to 50 mg per kg, and seizures at 60 mg per kg or more (Merck Veterinary Manual). Caffeine itself has a reported lethal dose of about 140 mg per kg. Caffeine pills and coffee beans pack a large dose into a small amount, so call poison control for any amount.

My dog ate coffee grounds. What should I do?

Take away what is left, note how much is missing, and call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661. Dry coffee grounds and beans hold a lot of caffeine, about 280 to 570 mg per ounce, so even a small mouthful matters for a small dog. Do not make your dog vomit unless a professional tells you to.

Is a lick of coffee dangerous for a dog or cat?

A lick or a sip is a very small dose, but this calculator never calls any amount safe. Brewed coffee holds roughly 20 mg of caffeine per fluid ounce at the high end, so a small cat or a toy dog can reach a harmful dose with a few ounces. If your pet drank more than a lick, or you are unsure, call poison control.

How much caffeine is toxic to a cat?

No cat-specific dose is established well enough to set a cutoff here. Reference tables list toxic doses for cats of about 80 to 150 mg per kg, and cats are somewhat more sensitive to methylxanthines than dogs. Because of that and because cats are small, this calculator treats any caffeine a cat swallows as an emergency.

Are energy drinks or caffeine pills worse than coffee?

Per spoonful or per pill, yes. Caffeine pills and pre-workout powders can hold 100 to 200 mg or more in one tablet, and Pet Poison Helpline notes that fatality is common when dogs ingest caffeine pills. Energy drinks can also contain xylitol or other sweeteners and added stimulants. Check the label and call poison control.
