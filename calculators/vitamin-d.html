Did Your Dog or Cat Eat Vitamin D Pills? Check the Risk.

Estimate the vitamin D dose for your pet’s size from the IU on the label and how many pills, gummies, or drops were swallowed.

Last reviewed November 27, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Too much vitamin D raises blood calcium and can damage the kidneys days later, even if your pet seems fine now.

High-strength vitamin D3 pills, gummies, and drops are a common cause of poisoning. Signs often take 12 to 48 hours to show, and treatment works best before then. Cats are more sensitive than dogs and have no established cutoff. This tool cannot call a dose safe. Contact a veterinarian or animal poison control after any amount.

Emergency Vet or Poison Control: Which Do I Call?

A vet may make your pet vomit if the pills were eaten recently, then check calcium and kidney values over the next few days.

Go Straight to an Emergency Vet If…

  • Your pet ate any psoriasis cream with calcipotriene or calcitriol
  • Your pet ate rat or mouse bait with vitamin D3
  • Your pet is a cat, or the result below says Emergency
  • Your pet is vomiting, very thirsty, or not eating

Call Poison Control First If…

  • Your pet just ate the pills and seems normal
  • You are unsure how many are missing
  • You want to know if your pet needs bloodwork
  • You want a case number to hand to your vet
Do not try to make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to.

How Vitamin D Poisoning Develops

Signs are delayed. A pet can look normal for a day or two while calcium is rising.

12 to 48 hours

Early signs

  • Vomiting, not eating
  • Weakness, tiredness
  • Drinking and peeing more
1 to 4 days

Calcium rising

  • Dehydration
  • Dark or bloody stool
  • Kidney injury on bloodwork
Severe cases

Organ damage

  • Kidney failure
  • Heart rhythm problems
Calcipotriene creams for psoriasis are far stronger than vitamin pills. Even a few licks from a tube can be serious.

Published Data Behind These Estimates

These dog figures come from the Merck Veterinary Manual chapters on cholecalciferol and on human supplements. 1 mg of vitamin D3 = 40,000 IU.

Vitamin D3 dose (dogs) Reported effect
0.1 mg/kg (4,000 IU/kg) Clinical signs may be seen at doses this low
0.5 mg/kg (20,000 IU/kg) Marked rises in calcium and phosphorus, with mineral deposits in soft tissues
13 mg/kg Reported acute lethal dose
Cats More sensitive than dogs; no established cutoff

How this calculator sorts results: for dogs, under 0.1 mg/kg = contact a professional now; 0.1 to under 0.5 mg/kg = Urgent; 0.5 mg/kg and above = Emergency. Any amount for a cat, any psoriasis cream with calcipotriene or calcitriol, and any vitamin D3 rodent bait = Emergency.

Common Questions

How much vitamin D is toxic to a dog?

Signs have been reported at about 0.1 mg per kg, which is 4,000 IU per kg. For a 20 lb (9 kg) dog that is about 36,000 IU, which can be a few high-strength pills or less than one 50,000 IU capsule.

My dog ate one vitamin D gummy. Is that an emergency?

It depends on the strength and your dog’s size, so enter both above. Either way, call poison control or your vet. Many gummies also contain xylitol, which is a separate emergency.

Why can my pet look fine after eating vitamin D?

Vitamin D raises blood calcium slowly. Signs often take 12 to 48 hours, and kidney damage can follow. Early treatment and bloodwork matter more than how your pet looks today.

Are psoriasis creams dangerous?

Yes. Calcipotriene and calcitriol creams are extremely potent forms of vitamin D. Any amount licked or chewed is an emergency.
