Did your dog eat chocolate? Check the risk by weight.

Estimate how urgently your dog needs veterinary attention after eating chocolate, based on body weight, chocolate type, and amount consumed.

Last reviewed September 8, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Chocolate type matters as much as the amount.

Baking chocolate contains roughly 10 times more theobromine than milk chocolate, gram for gram. A small amount of baking or dark chocolate can be more dangerous than a much larger amount of milk chocolate. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary toxicology data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control.

What to do right now

  1. Move any leftover chocolate and wrappers out of your dog's reach.
  2. Call your vet, ASPCA at (888) 426-4435, or Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661 now.
  3. Don't make your dog vomit unless a vet or poison control tells you to.
  4. Note the type of chocolate, about how much, and when, and keep the wrapper.
  5. Use the calculator below while you wait, then follow your vet's advice.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Both resources exist for a reason, and in most cases you should use both. Here is how to think about the difference.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your dog is already vomiting repeatedly, has a racing heartbeat, tremors, or seizures
  • It has been more than 2 hours since ingestion, so inducing vomiting at home is no longer reliable
  • Your dog has heart disease, is a puppy, senior, or small toy breed
  • The chocolate was baking chocolate, dark chocolate, or cocoa powder in any meaningful amount
  • A poison control consultation or your vet has told you to bring the dog in

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate the chocolate minutes ago and seems otherwise normal
  • You need help deciding whether the amount and type is dangerous for your dog's specific weight
  • You want a case number and treatment plan you can hand directly to your regular or emergency vet
  • You are unsure whether to induce vomiting at home, which should only be done under professional guidance
In practice, the fastest safe path is usually: call ASPCA Poison Control or Pet Poison Helpline while someone else drives toward the nearest open veterinary clinic. Do not wait to see if symptoms appear. Cardiac effects and seizures can develop even after early vomiting has already passed.

How theobromine toxicosis develops

Theobromine and caffeine, the two stimulant compounds in chocolate, are metabolized far more slowly by dogs than by humans, which is why even a moderate dose can cause a prolonged reaction.

6 – 12 hours

Early signs typically begin

  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea
  • Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
  • Restlessness or hyperactivity
  • Panting
Following hours (dose-dependent)

Cardiac and neurological signs

  • Elevated or abnormal heart rate at doses around 40–50 mg/kg
  • Tremors and seizures possible at doses of 60 mg/kg or higher
  • Elevated body temperature
  • Muscle rigidity
Up to 72 hours

Duration in severe cases

  • Clinical signs can persist up to 72 hours after ingestion due to slow theobromine metabolism in dogs
  • Ongoing monitoring is typically required through this window in significant ingestions
The reported lethal dose (LD50) of theobromine in dogs is 100–200 mg/kg, but severe signs and death have been documented at meaningfully lower doses in individual dogs. There is no dose considered guaranteed safe for a given dog, especially with baking or dark chocolate.

Published case data behind these estimates

These figures come from veterinary toxicology literature. They describe documented clinical thresholds, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous."

Common questions

How does this chocolate calculator estimate risk?

It uses your dog's weight, the type of chocolate, and the amount eaten to estimate a theobromine and caffeine dose, then compares that dose with published veterinary ranges. Baking and dark chocolate are far more concentrated than milk chocolate. The result is an urgency estimate, not a diagnosis, and it cannot rule out risk.

Is any amount of chocolate safe for a dog?

This page cannot call any amount safe. White chocolate has very little theobromine, but it can still upset the stomach, and many products labeled white are not pure. A small amount of baking chocolate can be more dangerous than a larger amount of milk chocolate. Call a veterinarian or poison control about any chocolate ingestion.

Should I make my dog vomit after eating chocolate?

Do not try to make your dog vomit unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to. Vomiting when a dog is already sedated or seizing can cause more harm. Call first, and keep the wrapper so they can see the type and the amount.
