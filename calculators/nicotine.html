Did your dog or cat eat nicotine? Check how much is toxic.

Estimate how urgently your dog or cat needs veterinary attention after eating cigarettes, cigarette butts, cigars, nicotine gum or lozenges, nicotine patches, or vape e-liquid and pods, based on species, body weight, the product, and how much was eaten.

Last reviewed October 29, 2026 by PetPoisonCalc against the sources listed below.

Nicotine acts fast, has a narrow safety margin, and has no antidote.

Signs can start within 15 to 30 minutes after a pet swallows vape liquid and within about 15 to 90 minutes after tobacco (Pet Poison Helpline). Clinical signs have been reported at doses as low as 1 mg per kg of body weight, and Pet Poison Helpline advises that home care is not appropriate even for small exposures. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control. Nicotine gum, lozenges, and some vape liquids can also contain xylitol, which is toxic to dogs on its own.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Nicotine works within minutes to an hour, so do not wait to see whether signs appear. Vets commonly induce vomiting if the pet has not already vomited and it is safe to do so, give activated charcoal, start IV fluids, and monitor heart rate, rhythm, and blood pressure. There is no antidote. The outlook improves a lot if a pet gets through the first 4 hours (Veterinary Medicine, 2004).

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your pet is vomiting, drooling heavily, shaking, wobbly, agitated, weak, collapsing, or having a seizure
  • Your cat ate any nicotine product
  • Your pet chewed a vape pod, cartridge, refill bottle, or a nicotine patch
  • The estimated dose is in the Emergency range

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate a small amount, such as one butt or one piece of gum, and seems normal
  • You are not sure of the product strength or how much is missing
  • The gum, lozenge, or liquid might also contain xylitol
  • You want a case number and treatment plan to hand to your vet
Do not make your pet vomit at home unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to. Nicotine often causes vomiting on its own, and seizures can start quickly. Do not give milk or antacids: antacids can increase how much nicotine is absorbed from the stomach.

How nicotine poisoning develops

Nicotine first overstimulates the nervous system, heart, and gut. At high doses that stimulation turns into blockade, which can lead to weakness, paralysis, and trouble breathing. Liquid nicotine is absorbed fastest; buffered gum and smokeless tobacco are also absorbed well through the mouth.

15 to 90 minutes

Early signs

  • Vomiting, drooling, diarrhea
  • Agitation, restlessness, fast breathing
  • Fast or slow heart rate, high blood pressure
First few hours

Nervous system and heart

  • Tremors, twitching, wobbliness
  • Abnormal heart rhythm
  • Seizures after larger doses
High doses

Depression and paralysis

  • Weakness, collapse, paralysis
  • Slow or stopped breathing, low blood pressure
  • Coma and death are possible
Signs may last a few hours after a minor exposure and more than 24 hours after a severe one (Pet Poison Helpline). Patches and gum can release nicotine for longer, so monitoring may be needed for several hours.

Published data behind these estimates

These figures come from veterinary toxicology references, poison control case data, and the FDA. Most toxic dose data for nicotine comes from dogs and from people; there is very little feline data. They describe documented effects, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous."

Nicotine dose or product Documented effect or content
1 mg/kg Clinical signs reported in pets at doses as low as this (Tobacco Control, 2011; MSPCA-Angell). The UK Veterinary Poisons Information Service treats 1 mg/kg as potentially toxic.
9.2 mg/kg (dogs) Reported minimum lethal oral dose in dogs (Tobacco Control, 2011). Other references give 9 to 12 mg/kg.
20 to 100 mg total Reported minimum lethal dose in dogs and cats: as little as one to five regular cigarettes (Veterinary Medicine, 2004; FDA)
Cats No established toxic threshold. Poison control cases in cats involved doses of about 1.7 to 170 mg/kg (Tobacco Control, 2011).
Cigarette / butt / cigar 9 to 30 mg per cigarette; 5 to 7 mg per butt; 15 to 40 mg per cigar (FDA; Veterinary Medicine, 2004)
Gum / patch / tobacco Gum 2 to 4 mg per piece; patches 8.3 to 114 mg each; chewing tobacco 6 to 8 mg per gram; snuff 12 to 16 mg per gram (Veterinary Medicine, 2004)
E-liquid About 8 to 59 mg per mL (Pet Poison Helpline). Up to 36 mg per mL in UK refills (VPIS).

This calculator uses, for dogs: 1 mg/kg and above = Emergency. Below 1 mg/kg = Urgent, except that a whole cigarette, more than one cigarette butt, a cigar, or any e-liquid, pod, or patch is always shown as Emergency, because the FDA calls those an emergency for pets. Doses of 9.2 mg/kg and above add a lethal-range warning. For cats, any amount is shown as Emergency, because no toxic threshold has been established for cats. There is no "safe" result, and even the lowest result says to call now.

Common questions

How much nicotine is toxic to a dog?

Clinical signs have been reported in dogs at doses as low as 1 mg of nicotine per kg of body weight, and 9.2 mg per kg is reported as the minimum lethal dose. A single cigarette butt (5 to 7 mg) is already more than 1 mg per kg for a dog under about 15 lb. Call poison control or your vet for any amount.

My dog ate a cigarette butt. What should I do?

Call your vet or ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 now, even if your dog seems fine. The FDA calls eating a whole cigarette or more than one butt an emergency. Signs usually start within an hour. Do not give milk or antacids, which can increase nicotine absorption.

Is nicotine gum dangerous for dogs?

Yes, for two reasons. Each piece holds 2 to 4 mg of nicotine, and many brands are sweetened with xylitol, which can cause a dangerous drop in blood sugar and liver injury in dogs. Check the label, run the xylitol calculator too, and call poison control.

Are vape pods and e-liquid worse than cigarettes?

Often, yes. Liquid nicotine is absorbed quickly, so signs can start within 15 to 30 minutes, and refill bottles and pods can hold a lot of nicotine. Pet Poison Helpline reports e-liquids from about 8 to 59 mg per mL. Treat any e-liquid ingestion as an emergency.

How much nicotine is toxic to a cat?

There is no established toxic dose for cats, so this calculator treats any nicotine a cat eats as an emergency. Cats in poison control case data were reported at doses of about 1.7 mg per kg and higher.
