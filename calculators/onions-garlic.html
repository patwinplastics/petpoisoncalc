Did your dog eat onion or garlic? Check the risk.

Estimate how urgently your dog needs veterinary attention after eating onions or garlic, based on body weight, food type, and amount consumed. Effects can take days to fully appear.

Last reviewed September 8, 2026 by the PetPoisonCalc editorial team against the sources listed below.

Symptoms from onion or garlic poisoning can take days to appear.

Unlike chocolate or xylitol, the red blood cell damage caused by onions and garlic often does not produce visible symptoms right away. Bloodwork changes can begin within 24 hours, but clinical signs of anemia frequently do not appear until 3 to 5 days later. A dog that looks fine today can still be at risk. This tool estimates relative urgency using published veterinary toxicology data. It cannot rule out risk, and every ingestion should be evaluated by a veterinarian or animal poison control.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Because effects are often delayed, the decision here is less about immediate collapse and more about getting ahead of a problem before it becomes severe.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your dog shows lethargy, pale or yellow (icteric) gums, weakness, rapid breathing, or dark urine, these can indicate hemolytic anemia already in progress
  • The amount eaten was concentrated (powder, dehydrated flakes, soup mix, or bouillon) even in a small total weight
  • Several days have passed since ingestion and your dog now seems unusually tired or is exercising less than normal
  • A poison control consultation or your vet has told you to bring the dog in

Call poison control first if…

  • Your dog just ate the food and currently shows no symptoms at all
  • You need help estimating whether the amount and type is dangerous for your dog's specific weight
  • You want guidance on what bloodwork (CBC, Heinz body smear) to request from your vet, and when
  • You want a documented case number and monitoring plan you can hand to your regular vet
Because red blood cell damage can take days to become clinically visible, a normal-looking dog after onion or garlic ingestion is not the same as a low-risk dog. If the estimated dose is meaningful for your dog's weight, follow up with bloodwork even if no symptoms appear right away.

How onion and garlic toxicosis develops

Sulfur-containing compounds in onions and garlic cause oxidative damage to red blood cells, forming Heinz bodies. This process unfolds over days, not hours, which makes it easy to underestimate.

Within 24 hours

Cellular changes begin

  • Heinz bodies (damaged hemoglobin clumps) begin forming and increasing in red blood cells
  • Methemoglobinemia may begin to develop
  • Usually no visible external symptoms yet
~72 hours

Peak methemoglobinemia

  • Methemoglobin levels typically peak around this point
  • Early signs may include lethargy or reduced appetite
3 – 5 days

Hemolytic anemia becomes apparent

  • Lethargy, weakness, reduced appetite
  • Rapid breathing and heart rate
  • Pale or yellow (icteric) gums
  • Dark or reddish urine (hemoglobinuria), which can also cause secondary kidney injury
  • Exercise intolerance, collapse in severe cases
Because clinical signs can lag several days behind ingestion, veterinarians often recommend a complete blood count and blood smear even in dogs that look outwardly normal after a meaningful onion or garlic ingestion.

Published case data behind these estimates

These figures come from veterinary toxicology literature. They describe documented clinical thresholds, not a line between "safe" and "dangerous."

Food and form Documented threshold in dogs
Raw onion ≈ 15–30 g per kg body weight
Garlic (raw, cooked, or powder) Approximately 3–5x more toxic than onion per gram
Concentrated forms (dehydrated flakes, powder, soup mix) Most dangerous per gram due to concentration

This calculator applies a conservative multiplier to garlic and to concentrated/powdered forms of both foods to reflect their significantly higher potency per gram compared to fresh, raw onion. When the exact form is uncertain, choose the more concentrated category for a more conservative estimate.
