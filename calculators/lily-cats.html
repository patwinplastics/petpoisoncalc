Did your cat touch a lily? Check if it is toxic.

Find out how urgently your cat needs veterinary care after chewing a lily, licking lily pollen, or drinking vase water. Lily type matters more than amount.

Last reviewed October 15, 2026 by the PetPoisonCalc editorial team against the sources listed below.

For cats, there is no safe amount of a true lily or daylily.

Eating a small piece of a leaf or petal, licking a few pollen grains off fur, or drinking vase water can cause fatal kidney failure in cats in less than 3 days. Treatment works best when it starts within hours, ideally before any signs. This tool is not a diagnosis. Call a veterinarian or animal poison control immediately for any possible lily exposure.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

With lilies, time is everything. Cats treated before kidney damage sets in usually do well; cats whose treatment is delayed beyond about 18 hours often develop irreversible kidney failure.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your cat chewed, ate, or licked any part of a true lily or daylily, or drank vase water
  • You do not know what type of lily it was
  • Your cat is vomiting, drooling, hiding, or not eating after being near lilies
  • Poison control or your vet has told you to come in

Call poison control first if…

  • You need help identifying the lily (take a photo or bring the plant)
  • The plant was a peace or calla lily and your cat seems normal
  • You want a case number to hand to the emergency vet
  • You can call while you are on the way to the vet
Do not wait for symptoms. Bring the plant or a photo of it, and wipe any pollen off your cat's fur and face with a damp cloth to prevent more grooming exposure.

How lily poisoning develops in cats

The toxin in true lilies and daylilies has not been identified, but it damages the cells of the kidney. It affects cats; dogs that eat lilies usually get only a mild stomach upset.

0 – 12 hours

Early signs

  • Vomiting, drooling
  • Low energy, hiding
  • Loss of appetite
12 – 24 hours

Kidney damage begins

  • Increased urination and thirst
  • Dehydration
  • Signs may seem to improve, which is misleading
24 – 72 hours

Kidney failure

  • Little or no urine
  • Weakness, collapse
  • Death without treatment
Peace lilies and calla lilies are not true lilies; they contain oxalate crystals that irritate the mouth (drooling, pawing at the mouth) but do not cause kidney failure. Lily of the valley contains heart toxins and needs urgent care in cats and dogs.

Published case data behind these estimates

These lists come from the U.S. FDA and ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center. Bring the plant or a photo to your vet to confirm the type.

Lily Risk to cats
Easter lily (Lilium longiflorum) Kidney failure — Emergency
Tiger lily (L. tigrinum / lancifolium) Kidney failure — Emergency
Asiatic lily and hybrids (L. asiaticum) Kidney failure — Emergency
Stargazer and Oriental lilies Kidney failure — Emergency
Rubrum / Japanese Show lily (L. speciosum) Kidney failure — Emergency
Wood lily (L. philadelphicum / umbellatum) Kidney failure — Emergency
Daylily (Hemerocallis spp.) Kidney failure — Emergency
Lily of the valley (Convallaria majalis) Heart rhythm problems — Emergency
Peace lily (Spathiphyllum), calla lily (Zantedeschia) Mouth and throat irritation, not kidney failure — contact a professional
Peruvian lily (Alstroemeria) Generally mild stomach upset — contact a professional
