There is no known safe amount of grapes or raisins for cats.

Grape and raisin kidney injury is well documented in dogs, but there are very few reports in cats: Merck Veterinary Manual mentions only anecdotal reports of kidney failure in cats, two cats with acute kidney failure were reported to the UK Veterinary Poisons Information Service, and even ASPCA sources disagree about cats. Because no safe or toxic dose has been established for cats, every result here is Emergency: contact a veterinarian or animal poison control now.

What to do right now

  1. Take away any grapes, raisins, or foods made with them, like trail mix, bread, or cookies.
  2. Call your vet or ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 now, even if your cat seems fine.
  3. Do not try to make your cat vomit at home. Hydrogen peroxide and salt are not safe for cats.
  4. Note what your cat ate, about how much, and when, and keep the packaging.
  5. If your vet says to come in, go now and bring the packaging with you.

Emergency vet or poison control: which do I call?

Because no safe or toxic dose has been established for cats, any amount is treated as an emergency. Calling early gives your vet the most options, such as inducing vomiting at the clinic when it is appropriate and checking kidney values.

Go straight to an emergency vet if…

  • Your cat is vomiting, will not eat, is very lethargic, or is drinking or urinating much more or much less
  • Your cat ate any amount of grapes, raisins, currants, or sultanas and your vet or poison control advises going in
  • Your cat has kidney disease or other health problems
  • You cannot tell how much was eaten

Call poison control first if…

  • Your cat ate a small amount and seems normal
  • You want to know whether your cat should be made to vomit at the clinic
  • You want a case number and treatment plan to hand to your vet
Never try to make a cat vomit at home. Hydrogen peroxide and salt are not safe for cats. Vets use specific medicines to induce vomiting in cats.

What to watch for after a cat eats grapes or raisins

Very few cat cases have been published, so this timeline combines the signs seen in reported cats with the better-documented course in dogs.

First 24 hours

Early signs

  • Vomiting (sometimes with pieces of fruit)
  • Lethargy
  • Diarrhea or loss of appetite
24 to 72 hours

Possible kidney injury (documented in dogs, reported in two cats)

  • Drinking and urinating more, or much less
  • Weakness, dehydration
  • Abdominal discomfort
Days 3 and later

Kidney failure if it develops

  • Little or no urine
  • Bad breath, mouth ulcers
  • Collapse
Early signs are those reported in the dogs and cats in the Dutch poison center study (2022). Kidney injury timing is from dog data (Merck Veterinary Manual).

Common questions

Are grapes and raisins toxic to cats?

Possibly. Grape and raisin kidney injury is well documented in dogs. In cats the evidence is limited: Merck Veterinary Manual mentions anecdotal reports of kidney failure in cats with no published case reports, the UK Veterinary Poisons Information Service has described two cats with acute kidney failure, and ASPCA sources disagree (one article says cats may also be affected, a blog post says grapes are toxic to dogs but not to cats). Because the risk cannot be ruled out, treat any ingestion seriously and call a vet or poison control.

How many grapes can a cat eat safely?

There is no known safe amount for cats. No toxic dose has been established, and in dogs some individuals have reacted to very small amounts. This calculator treats any amount as an emergency.

My cat ate one grape. What should I do?

Treat it as an emergency. Call your vet or ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 now. There is no established safe or toxic dose for cats, so even one grape or raisin cannot be called safe. Your vet may advise inducing vomiting at the clinic or monitoring kidney values.

What are the symptoms of grape poisoning in cats?

In the 2022 Dutch poison center study, signs reported in dogs and cats included vomiting, lethargy, diarrhea, and loss of appetite. In dogs, the main danger is acute kidney injury, which can show up as vomiting, reduced appetite, weakness, and drinking or urinating more or much less, usually within 24 to 72 hours.

Why do so few cats get grape poisoning?

Most cats are not interested in grapes or raisins, so exposures are rare and there is little data. A rare exposure is not proof of safety. The Dutch study found most cats had no signs, but 2 of 13 did, and the UK cases involved kidney failure.
